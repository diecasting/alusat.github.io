## Integrated Aluminum Extrusion Manufacturing

Our plant runs a complete extrusion value chain so accountability for quality never leaves the building:

- **Die design and tooling** — in-house extrusion die engineering, including a design-for-manufacturability review, so wall thickness, tolerances and die lines are resolved before steel is cut.
- **Extrusion** — aluminum extrusion presses spanning a broad tonnage range, producing solid, hollow, semi-hollow and multi-void profiles in both standard and thin-wall geometries.
- **Heat treatment** — aging ovens for T4, T5 and T6 tempers to reach the mechanical properties your application demands.
- **Cutting and fabrication** — precision sawing, drilling, tapping, bending, welding and assembly of extruded profiles into finished sub-assemblies.
- **CNC machining** — multi-axis CNC machining centers for milling, turning and complex features on aluminum profiles and components to tight tolerances.
- **Surface finishing** — anodizing (clear and color), powder coating, polishing and brushing for corrosion resistance and appearance.
- **Inspection** — CMM dimensional inspection, alloy verification and full production traceability on every order.

This vertical integration is what lets us quote realistically, hold lead times and keep tolerances consistent from first prototype to high-volume production.

## From Drawing to Delivered Part

A typical custom aluminum extrusion program at Alusat follows a predictable path:

1. **Feasibility and quoting** — you send a drawing, sample or sketch; our engineers review alloy, wall thickness, tolerances and finish, then return a feasibility assessment and quotation.
2. **Die development** — once the program is approved, we design and manufacture the extrusion die, with a design-for-manufacturability check before any steel is cut.
3. **Extrusion and aging** — billets are heated and extruded to the target profile, then aged to the required temper (T4, T5 or T6).
4. **Cutting and fabrication** — profiles are cut to length and, where needed, drilled, tapped, bent, welded or assembled.
5. **CNC machining** — features that cannot be extruded — pockets, threads, precision bores — are produced on CNC machining centers.
6. **Surface finishing** — anodizing, powder coating, polishing or brushing is applied to meet appearance and corrosion requirements.
7. **Inspection and packing** — CMM and dimensional checks confirm conformance, then parts are packed and documented for export.

Because every step is co-located, a design change that would normally bounce between several suppliers is resolved in one conversation.

## Extrusion Dies and Tooling

The die determines profile quality, so we invest in it up front. Alusat designs and manufactures solid, hollow and multi-void extrusion dies, choosing single- or multi-cavity layouts based on section size, wall thickness and annual volume. Die features such as bearing length and relief are tuned to control metal flow, reduce die lines and hold tolerances; for thin-wall or complex hollow sections this tuning is the difference between a profitable profile and a scrap-prone one. Standing tooling is stored, maintained and recertified between runs, so repeat orders start from a known-good die rather than a fresh setup.

## Custom Aluminum Profiles in the Alloys You Specify

Alusat extrudes the full range of wrought aluminum alloys used in structural, thermal and cosmetic applications:

- **6061** — the general-purpose structural alloy, easy to machine and weld, ideal for frames and brackets.
- **6063** — the architectural and decorative alloy, with excellent anodizing response for visible surfaces.
- **6005A** — a higher-strength structural alloy for transport and framing systems.
- **6082** — a strength-optimized alloy for demanding mechanical and structural parts.
- **7075** — the high-strength aerospace-grade alloy for performance-critical components.

Our engineers recommend the right alloy and temper for your load, thermal and cosmetic requirements, verify chemistry and temper before shipment, and document the result for traceability. Common tempers include T5 and T6 for structural parts and T4 where further forming is required after extrusion.

## Surface Finishing Options

Alusat offers the finishing routes most specified for aluminum extrusions:

- **Anodizing** — an electrolytic oxide layer, typically 5–25 µm, in clear, black or bronze. Anodizing improves corrosion and wear resistance and gives a durable, dye-stable appearance; it is the default for visible architectural and consumer surfaces.
- **Powder coating** — a cured polymer layer, typically 40–80 µm, in standard and custom RAL colors, with strong UV and chemical resistance for outdoor and industrial use.
- **Polishing** — mechanical or chemical polishing for a bright, reflective finish on decorative parts.
- **Brushing** — a satin, directional grain for a consistent non-glare surface.

Where a part needs both protection and color, we can combine processes — for example, anodize then dye, or chrome-free pretreat then powder coat — and will advise on the most cost-effective route for your environment and volume.
